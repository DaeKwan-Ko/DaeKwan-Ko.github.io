Dae Kwan Ko, Ph.D.

Causal and predictive regulatory genomics · Cell-type-resolved multi-omics · Functional genetics · Interpretable modeling

I am an Assistant Professor — Fixed Term in the Plant Research Laboratory , the Department of Plant Biology , and the Great Lakes Bioenergy Research Center (GLBRC) at Michigan State University.

My research program asks how cell-type-specific gene regulatory networks translate environmental change into adaptive plant responses, and whether experimentally supported regulatory rules can predict resilience across genotypes and environments. I integrate time-resolved transcriptomics, single-nucleus multiome profiling, transcription factor–DNA binding maps, functional genetics, molecular perturbation, and interpretable modeling to move from regulatory inference to causal testing and prospective prediction.

At MSU, I lead independent projects in sorghum environmental-stress genomics, regulatory-network inference, and network-enabled gene discovery. I serve as Principal Investigator on three competitive DOE Joint Genome Institute user projects and an MSU Project GREEEN award. My broader research is also supported through GLBRC within the Brandizzi Lab.

Research Program

Plants continuously adjust growth, physiology, and development as environments change, yet the regulatory mechanisms that determine whether these responses lead to adaptation or loss of performance remain incompletely understood. Genomic and single-nucleus technologies can now resolve transcription factor activity, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression at cell-type resolution, but most regulatory maps remain correlative. My program focuses on identifying which TF–cis-regulatory element–target relationships functionally control environmental responses and whether those experimentally supported rules remain predictive beyond the conditions in which they were discovered.

Central question: How do cell-type-specific regulatory networks translate environmental change into adaptive plant responses, and can experimentally supported regulatory rules predict resilience across genotypes and environments?

From regulatory maps to causal mechanisms

My research has progressed from mechanistic studies of Arabidopsis stress adaptation and recovery, through development of the network-enabled gene discovery framework NEEDLE, to integrated regulatory genomics in sorghum. This progression established a principle that now guides my laboratory: computational and genomic analyses are most informative when they generate mechanistic hypotheses that can be challenged experimentally and then used to improve prediction.

MAP → PERTURB → PREDICT

MAP

Define regulatory programs

Integrate time-resolved transcriptomes, DNA sequence and motif features, DAP-seq binding maps, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression to define cell-type-specific TF–cis-regulatory element–target relationships and distinguish shared from environment-specific programs across drought, heat, and salinity.

→
PERTURB

Establish causal mechanisms

Advance prioritized TF–CRE–target hypotheses through staged perturbation: transient TF–promoter assays and CRE mutagenesis, focused pooled CRISPR perturbation with single-cell transcriptomic readouts, and whole-plant validation for the strongest mechanisms.

→
PREDICT

Test generalization across contexts

Determine whether experimentally supported regulatory rules and natural variation in TFs and cis-regulatory elements predict molecular responses, physiology, growth, recovery, and plant performance across genotypes, environments, and genotype × environment combinations.

Modeling principles: interpretable machine-learning and network models; biologically structured holdouts in which entire environmental, temporal, or genetic contexts are excluded from training; prospective prediction rather than random partitioning of individual nuclei; and iterative model–experiment refinement in which both successful and failed predictions reveal biological structure.
environment + regulatory variation → regulatory state → physiological response → plant performance

Current Foundation

Drought · Heat · Salinity

Time-resolved stress transcriptomes

Root and shoot RNA-seq resources define shared, tissue-specific, and stress-specific transcriptional responses and provide temporal evidence for candidate regulatory programs.

142 stress-responsive TFs

Genome-scale TF–DNA binding atlas

Sorghum DAP-seq profiles define genome-scale TF-binding potential and a focused candidate space of TF–CRE–target relationships that can be evaluated in the context of chromatin accessibility, transcriptional activity, and environmental response.

RNA + chromatin in the same nucleus

Root single-nucleus multiome

Control and salinity-stressed sorghum roots reveal cell-type-specific transcriptional and chromatin-accessibility states, including distinct responses in cortex and pericycle populations.

Mechanism → model → experiment

Experimental and computational foundation

Arabidopsis stress-recovery genetics, NEEDLE, machine-learning-based network inference, promoter analysis, and functional assays establish a practical workflow for moving from genome-scale observations to experimentally validated regulatory mechanisms.

My long-term goal is to establish an experimentally grounded, predictive understanding of how gene regulatory networks enable plants to adapt to changing environments. As the program develops, I will extend MAP → PERTURB → PREDICT to stress recovery, combined environmental challenges, additional plant systems, and selected biotic interactions, asking which regulatory mechanisms are broadly conserved and which depend on genotype, cell type, or environmental history.

Research Support & Projects

My current research is supported through competitive user-facility awards, an independent MSU award, and collaborative GLBRC research. These projects have generated the multimodal resources that underpin my program in causal and predictive regulatory genomics of plant environmental adaptation.

Teaching & Mentoring

I teach students to connect biological mechanisms with quantitative evidence. My teaching emphasizes conceptual integration, experimental design, reproducible data analysis, responsible interpretation of computational models, and clear scientific communication.

Teaching Areas

  • Genetics and genomics
  • Plant molecular biology and stress physiology
  • Systems biology and gene regulatory networks
  • Bioinformatics and reproducible data analysis
  • AI/ML and quantitative regulatory genomics

Teaching Approach

I use active learning, structured discussion, primary literature, and real biological datasets to help students move from descriptive observations to testable models and evidence-based conclusions.

Mentoring

I mentor undergraduate researchers, research assistants, and early-career scientists through scaffolded training, explicit expectations, regular feedback, reproducible research practices, and meaningful ownership of scientific questions.

My goal is to help trainees become confident scientific thinkers who can work across experimental biology, computation, and agriculture.
